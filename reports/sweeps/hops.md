## At which hop the loop stops paying for itself

**Hop 2.** Read the marginal column: the first hop whose marginal ¥ per recall point is no lower than the previous hop's is the hop that no longer pays, so the loop's own break-even point is the hop before it. Recompute from the CSV if the numbers move; the rule is the column, not this sentence.

Arithmetic: marginal price of hop h = (¥/1k at h − ¥/1k at h−1) ÷ (recall@k at h − recall@k at h−1); the loop stops paying at the first h whose marginal price is ≥ the marginal price at h−1, or whose recall gain is zero.


## Reading this curve

Expectation, stated before the numbers: a second round recovers the questions whose first round surfaced nothing above the trigger; a third round recovers almost none of what the second did not, while still paying a rewrite call and an extra retrieval.

Saturation is read off the recall@k and citation-precision columns: the first point after which neither moves by more than the run-to-run noise (ADR-0012: a rerun reproduces its metrics through the cache) is where the swept parameter stops buying anything.
